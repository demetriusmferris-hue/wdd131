A long time ago, in a galaxy made of bricks

From screen
to studs.

Licensed sets translate memorable ships into buildable models. Here’s what the LEGO version captures—and what changes when a film vehicle becomes a toy.

Three sets, three perspectives

How close is the match?

These comparisons focus on recognizable design features, playability, and scale. LEGO set details vary by release; the listed numbers refer to the specific set shown.

01

Luke Skywalker’s X-wing Fighter · 75301

A starfighter built for the dogfight

On screen

The T-65 X-wing is a Rebel starfighter known for its distinctive four S-foils, astromech droid socket, and role in the attack on the first Death Star.

In the set

The 2021 play-scale model has 474 pieces, an opening cockpit, R2-D2 figure, and wings that open into attack position. Minifigure scale makes it compact beside the film ship.

What carries through: the X-shaped wings and blue-and-white Rebel markings are the unmistakable silhouette. The toy simplifies surface detail to keep the mechanism sturdy and playable.

02

Millennium Falcon · 75192

The Corellian freighter gets a collector-scale makeover

On screen

The Falcon is a heavily modified YT-1300 freighter. Its offset cockpit, circular radar dish, paired mandibles, and famously worn exterior set it apart from a pristine factory ship.

In the set

The Ultimate Collector Series model has 7,541 pieces and measures about 84 cm long. Removable hull panels reveal interior spaces, while interchangeable dishes reflect different film eras.

What carries through: asymmetry, recognizable hull shapes, and a layered greebled surface. The large model makes room for interiors that the film ship’s exterior alone cannot show.

03

The Razor Crest · 75331

A starship that tells a character’s story

On screen

The Mandalorian’s former gunship is practical and weathered rather than sleek. Its chunky engines, forward cockpit, and cargo hold reflect its life as a working transport.

In the set

The 2022 Ultimate Collector Series model has 6,187 pieces. Removable sections open up a detailed cabin, carbon-freezing chamber, and cargo bay alongside the ship’s broad silhouette.

What carries through: the sturdy, industrial profile. The large set gives interior storytelling more space than the camera usually has time to show.
