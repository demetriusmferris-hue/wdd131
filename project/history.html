A little history goes a long way

Built from a
good idea.

LEGO’s story begins in a small Danish workshop. Its name, materials, and brick design changed over time—but the invitation to imagine and build has endured.

A timeline of play

From wooden toys to a world of bricks

These milestones offer a concise starting point for a much bigger story. Dates and descriptions are drawn from the LEGO Group’s published company history.

  1. 1932

    A workshop in Billund

    Carpenter Ole Kirk Christiansen begins making wooden toys in Billund, Denmark.

  2. 1934

    A name with a purpose

    The company adopts the name LEGO, formed from the Danish phrase “leg godt,” meaning “play well.”

  3. 1949

    Plastic bricks arrive

    The company begins producing its early Automatic Binding Bricks, a plastic building toy that points toward the system still to come.

  4. 1958

    The modern brick takes shape

    A new brick design with tubes underneath is patented. The clutch between studs and tubes helps creations hold together while remaining easy to rebuild.

  5. 1968

    LEGO® House becomes a place

    LEGOLAND Billund opens, bringing the brick-built world into a theme park where visitors can explore models and attractions.

  6. 1999

    A galaxy joins the brick-built universe

    LEGO Star Wars sets debut, beginning a long-running collaboration that lets fans build spacecraft, characters, and scenes from the films.

One system, many possibilities

The 1958 design is often described as “clutch power”: the way a brick’s studs connect with the tubes of the brick above it. That connection makes a model sturdy, but still easy to take apart and reinvent.
